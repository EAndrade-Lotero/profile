### Artificial intelligence
{: #artificial-intelligence}

*Representación y solución automática de problemas.* Required course, 3 credits. Prerequisites: Probability and Statistics I, and Theory of Computation.

#### Summary

This course presents an overview of artificial intelligence. It is a starting point for discussing what intelligence is and how to build agents that act intelligently, and it introduces central topics in the field. Students examine the characteristics of an intelligent agent, how problems are represented, how optimal solutions are searched for, and how reasoning and inference systems work. By the end of the course, students understand the algorithmic foundations of artificial intelligence and how they relate to other areas of knowledge. The course also presents examples of AI applied in different fields.

#### Expected learning outcomes

By the end of the course, students are able to:

1. Describe contested questions about the ideal of artificial intelligence, and distinguish symbolic from subsymbolic AI and narrow AI from general AI.
2. Describe the main properties of the environments in which intelligent agents act.
3. Explain the main characteristics of an intelligent agent in the context of cognitive technologies.
4. Represent problems as state spaces and implement search algorithms for situations of different complexity.
5. Apply logic-based knowledge representation to carry out automatic inference in problems of low complexity.
6. Apply probabilistic knowledge representation to carry out probabilistic inference in problems of low complexity.
7. Design and implement an artificial neural-network architecture (autoregressive Transformers) and evaluate how well it simulates computation at the level of finite automata.

#### Contents

1. Introduction to artificial intelligence.
2. Search in state spaces.
3. Formal ontology and reasoning in predicate logic.
4. Automatic inference in classical logic.
5. Probabilistic inference: Bayesian networks and decision networks.
6. Two-player zero-sum games.
7. Connectionism, and subsymbolic versus symbolic processing.

### Reinforcement learning
{: #reinforcement-learning}

*Aprendizaje por refuerzo.* Elective, 2 credits. Prerequisite: Deep learning.

#### Summary

This course introduces reinforcement learning: its algorithms and its applications. By the end of the course, students are able to design and implement reinforcement-learning models to solve problems.

#### Expected learning outcomes

By the end of the course, students are able to:

1. Understand the conceptual approach of reinforcement learning.
2. Explain how different reinforcement-learning algorithms differ.
3. Implement reinforcement-learning models to solve problems.

#### Contents

1. The place of reinforcement learning in machine learning.
2. Markov decision processes and dynamic programming.
3. Monte Carlo methods and temporal-difference methods (Q-learning and SARSA).
4. Function approximation and deep reinforcement learning.
5. Policy-gradient methods.
